%--------------------------------------------------------
%	ABSTRACT
%--------------------------------------------------------
\Abstract{
In late-stage lung cancer, airway narrowing due to tumor growth or compression affects approximately 30\% of patients, necessitating precise stent selection for palliative care. Conventional imaging, such as CT scans, often fails due to obstructions or deformations, forcing clinicians to rely on subjective visual estimation from video bronchoscopy (VB) footage—an imprecise method prone to errors. This project explores a probe-based approach to estimate airway dimensions from VB footage, using a visible tool (e.g., biopsy forceps) as a scale reference to address the lack of intrinsic scale and challenges like poor image quality and distortions. A suite of experimental software tools was developed, including a simplified 3D tube experiment in Blender, achieving a mean error of 0.45 mm for circular cross-sections, and a computer vision pipeline to detect C-shaped tracheal cartilages in real VB images. Additionally, two PyQt5-based GUI applications were created: one for manual polygon annotation to compute dimensions and another for interactive probe placement and scaling. Preliminary results show sub-millimeter accuracy in controlled settings and successful cartilage detection, offering a proof of concept for computer-assisted stent planning in complex cases where traditional imaging is unreliable, though further validation is needed.


}
